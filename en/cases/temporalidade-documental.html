Case 02 · 1Doc

Document retention schedules

How we brought 1Doc to cover the full lifecycle of public records, from the retention schedule to final disposition, turning complex archival regulations into a clear digital flow for government agencies.

01

Context

1Doc already offered part of a records management toolset through its Classification Plan, linked to each subject. However, the platform did not yet support the retention and disposition workflow, a functional gap compared with competitors in the market.

This opened an opportunity to evolve the product, extending support for the lifecycle of public records and meeting agencies’ needs around classification, retention periods and final disposition.

02

Problem

Until now, 1Doc kept every single process issued on the platform, with no way to handle records disposition, something oversight bodies require. The Classification Plan existed, but classified documents followed no lifecycle: there were no retention periods, no appraisal and no disposal.

In practice, municipalities couldn’t carry out proper records management, and every document was stored indefinitely.

03

Process and discovery

CSD matrix

We started with a CSD matrix (Certainties, Suppositions and Doubts) to surface the rules and open questions around records disposition in the public sector: which laws apply, who approves disposal, and what has to happen before a document can be destroyed.

Competitive benchmarking

We mapped competitors’ features to understand how the market handled records retention, which steps made up their workflows and which capabilities could inspire 1Doc’s evolution.

Mapping the SEI workflow

We mapped the entire workflow of SEI (the Brazilian federal government’s electronic records system) to make sure 1Doc would offer, at a minimum, what this free federal software already gives municipalities.

Requirements gathering

We researched and structured the rules the federal government requires for records disposition, understanding each step, condition, dependency and edge case. The goal was to turn a complex process governed by archival regulations into clear requirements for a digital solution tailored to the platform’s module- and issuance-based model.

04

Solution

The design built on 1Doc’s legacy interface. That decision was driven by the complexity of the business rules and the urgency of the launch, since the platform redesign is still under way. The proposal covers the main stages of the records retention lifecycle, from setting retention periods to final disposition.

  1. Records retention schedule. Setting up retention periods for the active and semi-active phases, plus the final disposition of each document class, according to each agency’s rules.
  2. Link to the Classification Plan. The rules connect to the codes and classes of the existing plan. The solution builds on the current structure and extends it, tying classification to how long documents are kept and how they are disposed of.
  3. Disposition queue. Brings together the items eligible for disposal or permanent retention, with a consolidated view of what needs to be appraised.
  4. Disposal lists. Users select eligible documents and processes, organize them into formal lists and review everything before submitting it for approval.
  5. CPAD approval. The workflow automatically generates the records of official acts and routes items to the committee for signature at each step. It also lets agencies publish the notices directly in 1DOM, 1Doc’s Official Gazette, if they have the product.
  6. Final disposition. Once approved, users carry out the disposal of authorized items or transfer documents to permanent retention.
  7. History and traceability. A record of everything that was disposed of or transferred to the archive, including the items and the steps taken, supporting transparency and accountability to oversight bodies.
05

Validation

Using a working prototype built with the help of Claude, we validated the flow with a 1Doc sales analyst who holds a degree in archival science, to confirm that every archival rule was met.

We also ran 4 interviews with 1Doc customers: admin users who sit on their municipality’s CPAD (Permanent Document Appraisal Committee). The flow was validated with strong approval.

06

Why this delivery matters

The delivery brings 1Doc closer to legal requirements and the SIGAD model, and sets it apart from tools that only classify.

§

Legal compliance in tenders

Law No. 8,159/1991 and e-ARQ Brasil require public agencies to keep a Classification Plan and a Retention Schedule. 1Doc now meets this within the platform itself.

◎

Closer to SIGAD

The platform used to only classify. Now it calculates due dates, builds the queue of what needs appraisal and organizes the lists, moving closer to archival management guidelines.

▲

Ahead of competitors

Players without a Retention Schedule or automatic search for expired items fall behind. Less manual work, stronger legal compliance and a strong differentiator in the public sector.

Results
